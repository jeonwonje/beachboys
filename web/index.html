The problem

The tide never stops delivering.

Most of what lands on Singapore's beaches arrives from the sea, so a clean beach only stays clean until the next tide. On Sentosa that means crews working the shoreline by hand, every day.

97%of beach waste comes from offshore Straits Times, NEA data, Jan 2025
~20workers clean Sentosa's beaches Site interview, Apr 2026

The gap

Sieves stop working where the sand gets wet.

Wet grains stick to each other and to the wires, so a screen blinds instead of sifting. The highlighted strip is the wet band between the tide marks. It has no machine fallback, so workers bend, pile and lift it by hand, in the heat.

We went looking for a narrower problem than "clean the beach". This strip is it: pick items up off wet ground, without a gravity sieve.

How we got here

Twelve months of ideas failing cheaply.

The first idea was a whole robot. It was rejected inside a month. Almost everything since has been a smaller thing that broke, and told us what to do next.

  1. Nov 2025

    A whole beach robot

    Our first proposal: a full machine with a 50 L bin. Our supervisor's challenge was blunt: don't build another platform. Build the part existing robots are missing.

    Rejected
  2. Mar 2026

    Cardboard, by hand

    Scoops just pushed debris forward. A moving belt with spokes actually lifted it. That one afternoon chose the architecture.

    Conveyor wins
  3. Mar 2026

    Screws and sieves

    An Archimedes screw clogged in wet sand. A vibrating mesh box did poorly. A hand-spun mesh drum dropped most of its wet sand in one turn.

    Drum wins
  4. Apr 2026

    Interim prototypes

    Bench rigs for each function. Conveyor current rose from about 0.3 A to 0.9 A as the spokes dug into dry sand. Seaweed came up, then tangled.

    Measured
  5. Jun 2026

    Finding a carrier

    The test robot got stuck in sand within about a minute. We borrowed a Copernicus rover with a dead control board and bypassed it, driving the motors directly over RS-485.

    The repair, on GitHub
  6. 5 Aug 2026

    First wet sand

    The bare carrier drove onto wet sand at West Coast. So the platform was settled, and everything else had to be built by October.

    It drives
  7. Sep 2026

    Cut the drum in half

    A full drum left nowhere for the conveyor to reach into, so light items fell beside it. Removing the top half fixed the handoff. It now rocks instead of spins.

    Half-drum

The machine

Three subsystems on a borrowed rover.

The module rides on two 4040 rails on a Botsync Copernicus. Each subsystem comes off by hand on 10 mm quick-release pins, because wet sand packs into everything and someone has to clear it at the end of a shift.

  • Conveyor lifts debris, with the sand around it
  • Half-drum shakes wet sand off the debris
  • Tray and bin keep sand and debris apart
  • Copernicus drives, by remote control

Drag to look around. This is the team's real model, repainted to match the photos. Overall 1630 × 726 × 926 mm.

Stage 1 · Built by Russell and Yizhang

Conveyor

Hooked spokes on a mesh belt dig under debris and carry it uphill. Stage one doesn't try to separate anything. Loose sand just drops through the belt on the way up.

Skid
Cut from 7 to 5 cm, then locked, because free-pivoting skids blocked the machine
Spokes
Printed hooks on a 350 mm belt with 10 mm aperture
Drive
24 V, 40 W gearmotor, 3:1 timing belt. The 1:1 drive stalled
Paddle
Passive tri-flap, added after large light items got pushed ahead of the intake
Mount
Two 10 mm quick-release pins, no tools

Stage 2 · Built by Ming Yuan and Gary

Half-drum

A flat sieve clogs on damp sand. So the half-drum throws the load against its mesh, and the knocks free the sand that gravity alone wouldn't.

Screen
5 mm aluminium mesh between two halved 34 cm bicycle rims
Carriers
Inline skate wheels sized to the rim channel. Castors sat loose and let it wander
Drive
Sprung friction wheel, on its fourth try after capstan rope and GT2 belt both slipped
Stroke
Rocks between two limit switches, coasting 150 ms at each end

Stage 3 · Built by Gary and Ming Yuan

Tray and bin

Sand falls through the screen onto a tray, and debris carries on over the back into a bin. One worker lifts a single bin rather than dozens of piles.

V plate
Acrylic, keeps sand off the drum motor and steers it to the tray
Tray
1 mm aluminium, about 2.9 L
Bin
Mesh, at the rear, where it counterbalances the conveyor
Pins
Moved to the rear after the first mount bent under a 10 kg load

Power and control · Built by Wonje

Its own battery, its own brain.

The carrier's spare 24 V rail turned out to be a current-limited fan output with no published rating. So the module got a separate 24 V, 504 Wh sodium-ion pack, and can be built, tested and removed without touching the rover.

Controller
Arduino Mega 2560. Replaced a Raspberry Pi, whose 3.3 V logic didn't suit the driver
Driver
XY160D dual H-bridge, about 7 A per channel
Switches
Normally closed, so a broken wire reads the same as a hit and the drum stops
Runtime
Roughly 6 h at about 80 W Nameplate estimate

In operation

One pass.

  1. Collect. An operator drives along the wet band. The skid sets a shallow dig, and the spokes lift debris with its sand.
  2. Separate. The half-drum rocks the load. Wet sand falls through onto the tray.
  3. Store. Debris rolls on into the bin, ready to tip out at the utility vehicle.
